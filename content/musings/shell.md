The smoke and heat was getting intolerable day by day. She couldn’t take it anymore. Things didn’t get any better with the continuous cacophony of the traffic. It was already 9.30 am, she was going to miss her morning attendance in college for sure. The Uber driver tried to fight his way through the traffic. But even a two wheeler couldn’t slip past the morning Calcutta traffic. She was wrapped up in a white scarf, all over her face and neck. Protecting a fair skin in the summer of Calcutta was indeed a difficult task.

 It was 9.55 by the time she reached the campus. With still 15 minutes at hand, she rang her friend and joined the circle with a coffee. Coffee was going to keep her up today in class. Returning late from a social campaign last night, she barely managed 5 hours of sleep.

She was into all these social things a lot. This last campaign was a all-women camp, to raise awareness against verbal harassment centred around body shaming. She had once spotted her sister getting bullied because of her complexion: your sister is so pretty, how come you look like this? She couldn’t say anything then, just stood by afar, while her sister was getting insulted. To be totally honest, she felt an inkling of pride that people noticed her, noticed their differences. Given the situation, anyone would pick her over most other girls anyway, she used to think. 

She had a dedicated Instagram for these social causes. Right now, three pictures were ready to be posted. One was her, hugging a girl, her back towards a few other people laughing about something. The tag would be “She isn’t five, stop treating her like one. What does her physical growth matter anyways?”, she thought. The next picture was a little boy, crying his eyes out. The kid had lost his hair due to chemotherapy. Now kids at school patted him and made fun of him. She would think of a tagline later. The last one was  a poster, of how the organization helps fighting bodyshaming and she is an active volunteer there. 

Her friends were talking to a student, by the time she found them. “Who’s he?”, she asked her friends. “Don’t know” they said. Followed by, “This is college, kid. We don’t have fifth grade here. Are looking for your way to school?”, to the student. The guy had his head hung, his road blocked by the girls. They started laughing at him. “He really must have lost his way. Kids!”, she said. 

Soon it was time to go to class. Class started with a  shock for everyone. “I heard, it’s terrible. Why would she do it?”, said one student of class. Slowly bits and pieces of conversation floated to her. A girl had committed suicide yesterday. A note was found as well: just too good. No one knew what it meant. She didn’t know the girl well, “Is it that fat girl with short hairs?”  She asked her friends. People nodded. She thought she might feel sorry. But she didn’t. How can you feel sorry for someone you barely know?, she thought. Must have been issues with her boyfriend, one of the students of the class said. A guy corrected him, “Girl didn’t have a boyfriend. No offense, but I would like to date someone better looking atleast” and winked at her. She winked back. He was a nice guy. 

Class was about to start. She was scrolling her instagram. Saw one post on “judge by the soul, not skin” , hit a like, turned off her phone and started writing her notes.
