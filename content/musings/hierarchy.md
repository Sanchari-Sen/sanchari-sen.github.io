His parents were asleep in the next room. One at night and it was their sleepover after a long time. It was supposed to be ‘fun’. They were fifteen, so why not?. In the darkness of the room, the computer screen lit up and the boys huddled over it. Giggles and fits of laughter followed. “Fun, Nate?”, someone asked. More giggles. Well it is all a part of growing up, isn’t it?

In the faint red light of her room, she lay sprawled on the floor. Clothes all bloody and torn to pieces. She was crying, not from pain but because the ugly marks on her skin will reduce her demand now. How will she feed herself? There was no mercy in the House. Even tonight she was starving. Refusing a customer is not acceptable here. But she had to; she was feeling sick today, not quite okay to ‘entertain’ him. Hence the punishments-merciless beating and starvation. She had vomited thrice since morning, and the nausea kept increasing. Even now she was slipping in and out of consciousness. Last night she even had dreams of the day when she entered the House first. The lady at the counter had made her sign a paper, her life has been theirs since. She was barely thirteen then, three years back. She remembered how she had been attacked suddenly one day in the alley, tied, masked and stung with some liquid in the neck. Life had been different altogether since then. No school, no homework, no friends, just being with her customers whenever they wanted serving the House. She heard them say how valuable an asset she was. But she did not like her life here, did not find herself any valuable or worthy. 

The nausea was getting worse. She didn’t even bleed last month. Her fears might be coming true. A doctor had been called. He gave her some pills, advised rest. That was probably not possible though. The counter lady will decide that. She often saw her customers smiling at the counter. When was the last time she smiled, she did not remember. All she remembered was pain. 

A little girl walked up to the counter. Barely twelve. “Where’s momma?” she asked. “On duty”, the counter lady replied. She was new here, newer than the child at least. The child had grown up here. Five minutes later, the door at the end of the corridor opened. A tall man walked out, she followed, fixing her hair and went to her child. He signed his name at the counter- Nate Stone, and left. The child looked at her mom, the question evident in her eyes- did he hurt you? She shook her head no with a sad smile. It was always a ‘no’. The counter lady looked at the child and said “How much longer?” Well not much. It would be her life soon, just like her mother. She couldn’t escape it, because did not know a life could exist beyond those walls.
